Metrics and Reporting Framework

When I became manager of the Digital Content team, there was no scalable way to measure productivity, track progress, or demonstrate value to leadership. Without metrics, forecasting was impossible, and the organization lacked visibility.

I needed to create a reliable, data-driven system that could track performance, support forecasting, and provide leadership with actionable insights.

I designed and implemented the organization's first metrics framework using JIRA. This included building customized boards, automated queries, and role-specific dashboards that cLarified priorities, standardized workflows, and enabled real-time performance tracking. I also established a reporting cadence and shared results with the VP, demonstrating the strategic value aof measurable operational insight.

The success of this model led the VP to adopt it across all teams, ultimately evolving into an org-wide metrics portal used for executive reporting. This initiative introduced a culture of transparency, enabled accurate capacity planning, and became the foundation for performance measurement at scale.

Architecture Diagram Standards

As the sole team responsible for creating all architecture diagrams for the Oracle Architecture Center, I identified significant inconsistencies in diagrams produced across various Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) product teams. This resulted in confusion for users and an unpolished representation of the OCI brand.

I needed to establish a unified set of enterprise-wide standards that governed how architecture diagrams should be designed, documented and delivered for publication on Oracle.com marketing pages, Oracle Help Center's Architecture Center, and marketing assets. To deliver on this task, I:

  • Identified and assembled a cross-functional group of SMEs, including architects, PMs, and VPs, to define expectations and build alignment.
  • Led recurring review sessions to reach consensus on structure, terminology, iconography, hierarchy, and accessibility requirements.
  • Documented comprehensive standards and created scalable templates, reference examples, and usage guidelines.
  • Built and maintained all standards in FIgma, draw.io, and PowerPoint (PPT) so the design team had fully featured component libraries while product managers and architects had accessible, easy-to-use templates.
  • Trained product teams, onboarded new contributors, and partnered with leadership to ensure organization-wide adoption.

This resulted in the first unified architecture diagram standards for OCI, which significantly improved clarity and consistency across all published diagrams. The availability of standards in Figma, draw.io and PowerPoint enabled broad adoption across both technical and non-design teams, reduced revision cycles, and strengthened Oracle's technical brand throughout the Architecture Center.

Oracle Learning YouTube Governance Overhaul

When my team assumed ownership of Oracle's largest YouTube channel, I discovered critical security gaps and an inconsistent publishing workflow. Dozens of developers shared a single login tied to a legacy APEX application, resulting in security vulnerabilities, no traceability, and inconsistent compliance with Brand and accessibility standards.

I led a cross-functional effort with Documentation, Marketing, and Corporate Security to redesign the entire publishing model. I eliminated shared logins and introduced secure interim individual access while a permanent authentication solution was developed.

Because the organization lacked resources for a dedicated intake team, I created a scalable certification-based publishing program. The training covered security protocols, medadata standards, accessibility requirements, and brand rules, and required a perfect score to gain publising permissions. This enabled decentralized publishing with centralized compliance.

To improve content governance, I enhanced our ownership tracking process, ensuring every uploaded video could be tied to an accountable owner.

The result was significant across security, operations, and brand governance. By eliminating shared logins and establishing a certification-based publishing model, I closed a major corporate security vulnerability and ensured that every upload to Oracle’s largest YouTube channel had clear ownership and accountability. The new workflow created consistent compliance with brand, accessibility, and metadata standards, strengthening Oracle’s external digital presence. The self-service certification approach enabled scalable publishing without additional headcount, reducing operational risk while improving efficiency and quality control. Ultimately, this governance model elevated the professionalism, safety, and reliability of Oracle Learning’s content ecosystem and became a reference point for broader content governance efforts within the company.

Enterprise Iconography System

Oracle had no formal, accessible, or documented standards for icon creation, leading to inconsistencies, long turnaround times, and a reliance on Brand Marketing and an external vendor for icon requests. I led a multi-phase initiative to standardize iconography across the enterprise, eliminate bottlenecks, and build a scalable system that empowered designers while strengthening brand integrity.

As the leader of a global creative team, I needed to eliminate the bottleneck, establish a unified system for brand-consistent iconography, empower designers across Oracle to create compliant icons, and centralize distribution so teams could easily access approved visual assets.

I initiated deep collaboration with Brand Marketing, meeting repeatedly with their designers to fully understand their icon creation principles—everything from grid structure and stroke behavior to accessibility constraints and motion guidelines. My team and I meticulously documented these standards in a clear, structured, and scalable format that surpassed what Brand Marketing had internally.

I then created a full iconography system, including:

  • Comprehensive documentation with visual examples, do/don’t guides, and accessibility notes
  • A training program to upskill designers across product and creative teams
  • A streamlined request workflow enabling teams to submit requests directly to my group rather than through Brand Marketing
  • To improve accessibility and enterprise-wide adoption, I uploaded all approved icons into Figma and Oracle Media Manager (OMM), giving every Oracle team a single source of truth for iconography.

Additionally, I established a formal intake process that allowed product teams to request new service icons from my team rather than routing them through Oracle’s external vendor. This not only reduced turnaround time but also created significant cost savings by bringing icon creation in-house.

The documentation and systems were so well-executed that a graphic designer who transferred from Brand Marketing to my team told me that our documentation was “far better than anything Brand had ever produced.” Designers across Oracle were finally equipped to create brand-consistent icons independently, turnaround times dropped significantly, and product teams no longer needed to outsource icon creation—saving money and improving speed and consistency.

SharePoint Migration

Oracle historically relied on Oracle Content Management and Confluence for internal documentation and content storage. In 2024, Oracle announced an enterprise-wide transition to Microsoft OneDrive and SharePoint. This shift required every team to migrate their content, restructure their documentation approach, and align with new corporate standards while ensuring no disruption to stakeholders who depended daily on our assets, diagrams, video files, and process documentation.

I was responsible for transitioning my team’s entire content ecosystem (file storage, diagrams, architecture documentation, wikis, and internal websites) to Microsoft OneDrive and SharePoint. My goal was not only to move files but to create a unified, intuitive, customer-facing hub where internal stakeholders could access our deliverables, processes, and standards from a single, reliable link. To accomplish this, I:

  • Conducted a full audit of all existing assets across Oracle Content Management, individual computers, and Confluence-based documentation.
  • Designed the information architecture for our new SharePoint sites, ensuring intuitive navigation, clear labeling, and alignment with our team workflows.
  • Migrated all files to OneDrive using a structured folder taxonomy to eliminate duplication, streamline access, and reduce search time.
  • Rebuilt documentation, process pages, and guidance from Confluence into SharePoint using modern pages, web parts, and integrated permissions.
  • Consolidated all customer-facing materials into one central SharePoint site, reducing confusion caused by fragmented links and outdated repositories.
  • Trained my team on updated processes, file-management best practices, and how to maintain the SharePoint site going forward.

The migration resulted in a fully modernized, centralized content ecosystem that was delivered ahead of schedule with no interruption to customers or internal teams. By consolidating all processes, documentation, and deliverables into a single SharePoint site, I eliminated the confusion caused by scattered repositories and outdated links. The new structure significantly improved usability and reduced the time required to locate files and instructions. This unified hub enhanced transparency, strengthened alignment with Oracle’s new enterprise standards, and provided a scalable model that other teams began referencing for their own transitions.
