Enterprise Standards and Cross-Org Transformation

OCI Architecture Diagram Standards

As the only team responsible for producing architecture diagrams for the Oracle Architecture Center on the Oracle Help Center, we quickly realized that diagrams submitted by product teams and architects were highly inconsistent. Styles, icon usage, terminology, and structure varied widely, which created confusion for customers and diluted Oracle’s brand credibility. OCI leadership recognized the need for unified standards and assigned ownership of this initiative to me.

I was tasked with establishing a comprehensive, authoritative set of OCI architecture diagram standards—governing visual design, terminology, icon usage, hierarchy, formatting, accessibility, and brand alignment—and implementing these standards across the organization.

I began by identifying and assembling the right subject matter experts, pulling together a cross-functional working group that included principal architects, product managers, and multiple VPs. I led this group through recurring review sessions to define requirements, resolve conflicting perspectives, and establish consensus on rules that balanced technical accuracy with visual clarity.

I documented the standards from the ground up, covering structure, workflows, color usage, callouts, components, icon libraries, naming conventions, accessibility guidelines, and file management. I then operationalized the standards by creating templates, Figma libraries, examples, and reference guides. I evangelized the guidelines across OCI, training teams and working directly with product groups to ensure proper adoption.

The result was the first unified, enterprise-wide architecture diagram standard for OCI. It dramatically improved consistency across all Architecture Center diagrams, strengthened Oracle’s technical brand identity, streamlined the content review process, and enabled faster internal collaboration. The standards became the foundation for all future architecture documentation and are still in use across OCI today.

Oracle Learning YouTube Governance and Publishing Certification

In 2022, my team inherited sole ownership of Oracle’s largest YouTube channel, Oracle Learning, which served millions of viewers. I quickly identified major security and operational risks. The login credentials were shared broadly with anyone who had admin access to the Oracle Learning Library APEX application, meaning multiple developers logged into the same YouTube account with a single shared password. Not only was this extremely unsecure, but it also made it impossible to identify who uploaded what videos or confirm adherence to security and brand standards.

I was responsible for securing the channel, establishing publishing governance, and creating a scalable system that ensured brand quality, content accuracy, and compliance—without adding headcount or delaying content delivery across Oracle.

I partnered with executives across documentation, marketing, and corporate security to assess the risks and agree on a shared governance model. I redesigned the entire publishing workflow:

  • Security Overhaul:
    I eliminated the shared-login model and introduced temporary individual logins as a stop-gap while a more robust system was built by engineering.
  • Ownership Tracking Improvements:
    I enhanced video ownership tracking in our internal database and created processes that increased accuracy and accountability, even before technical automation was available.
  • Certification Program:
    Because we lacked resources to staff a dedicated intake/publishing team, I developed a certification-based model.I created a mandatory internal training program that taught developers publishing standards, security requirements, accessibility expectations, and metadata rules.I built an exam that required 100% accuracy to ensure compliance before anyone could publish to the channel.
    This created scalable governance without adding personnel.
  • Cross-Functional Alignment:
    I secured alignment and approval from legal, security, documentation leadership, and marketing to ensure the new model met all corporate requirements.

The new governance model transformed the way Oracle Learning content was published:

  • Eliminated a major corporate security risk.
  • Achieved full traceability and accountability for all uploads.
  • Ensured 100% compliance with brand, accessibility, and publishing standards.
  • Reduced operational burden by creating a self-service, training-backed publishing pipeline.
  • Established a scalable model that supported the largest channel at Oracle without additional headcount.

This initiative became a template for future content governance discussions across Oracle's digital ecosystems.

Enterprise Iconography System

Oracle lacked formally documented iconography standards. Brand Marketing was the recommended owner, but they were overwhelmed—resulting in long turnaround times and inconsistent icons produced by designers across multiple teams. With no central repository or clear guidelines, product areas were using icons that did not align with Oracle's brand identity, creating fragmentation across the enterprise.

As the leader of a global creative team, I needed to eliminate the bottleneck, establish a unified system for brand-consistent iconography, empower designers across Oracle to create compliant icons, and centralize distribution so teams could easily access approved visual assets.

I initiated deep collaboration with Brand Marketing, meeting repeatedly with their designers to fully understand their icon creation principles—everything from grid structure and stroke behavior to accessibility constraints and motion guidelines. My team and I meticulously documented these standards in a clear, structured, and scalable format that surpassed what Brand Marketing had internally.

I then created a full iconography system, including:

  • Comprehensive documentation with visual examples, do/don’t guides, and accessibility notes
  • A training program to upskill designers across product and creative teams
  • A streamlined request workflow enabling teams to submit requests directly to my group rather than through Brand Marketing

To improve accessibility and enterprise-wide adoption, I uploaded all approved icons into Figma and Oracle Media Manager (OMM), giving every Oracle team a single source of truth for iconography.

Additionally, I established a formal intake process that allowed product teams to request new service icons from my team rather than routing them through Oracle’s external vendor. This not only reduced turnaround time but also created significant cost savings by bringing icon creation in-house.

The documentation and systems were so well-executed that a graphic designer who transferred from Brand Marketing to my team told me that our documentation was “far better than anything Brand had ever produced.” Designers across Oracle were finally equipped to create brand-consistent icons independently, turnaround times dropped significantly, and product teams no longer needed to outsource icon creation saving money and improving speed and consistency.

This project created Oracle’s first scalable, accessible, and enterprise-wide iconography system—strengthening brand cohesion, accelerating design workflows, and reducing reliance on external vendors.

Asset Repository Modernization

My creative team managed thousands of visual assets (icons, graphics, diagrams, animations) but they were scattered across individual computers. Whenever someone needed an asset, they had to ask the entire team if anyone had it. This slowed production, caused delays across multiple projects, and often led to duplicate asset creation because no one had visibility into what already existed.

I needed to implement a centralized, scalable asset repository that would allow my team, and eventually teams across Oracle, to store, search, reuse, and share assets efficiently. The system needed to support our existing workflows, maintain brand consistency, and integrate with enterprise standards.

I conducted a comprehensive vendor search, evaluating multiple digital asset management (DAM) solutions for security, searchability, access control, scalability, and budget alignment. Midway through the evaluation, our org was moved, and leadership asked me to explore internal options before procuring new software.

I connected with Oracle’s Brand Marketing organization and discovered they were using an internal DAM solution built on OpenText. I partnered with their administrators to assess capabilities, ensure it met our team’s needs, and align taxonomies and metadata standards. My team then collaborated closely with the repository admins to configure folder structures, naming conventions, and metadata tagging.

Once configured, we began systematically uploading, categorizing, and distributing our assets. I led the rollout of onboarding, documentation, usage standards, and communication to cross-functional teams so they could access and leverage our assets company-wide.

The new repository eliminated duplicated work, reduced turnaround time for asset retrieval, and improved workflow efficiency across multiple teams. Instead of sending messages and waiting hours or days for someone to locate a file, teams could instantly search and retrieve assets. This increased overall productivity and strengthened brand alignment company wide. It also positioned our team as a shared services provider for visual assets across Oracle.

Figma Migration and Design System for Technical Diagrams

My team was responsible for creating Oracle’s architecture and technical diagrams used across documentation, training, and marketing. These assets were built in Adobe Illustrator, which made version control difficult, slowed collaboration, and created inconsistency across designers. Although many other Oracle teams were beginning to adopt Figma, I was initially reluctant to switch tools because Illustrator was deeply embedded in our workflow and the diagrams were highly specialized.

I needed to determine whether Figma could meaningfully improve our architecture-diagram production process without disrupting quality or timelines. The goal was to evaluate the tool objectively, identify potential benefits, and, if it proved valuable, lead the transition in a way that unified our design workflows and enabled cross-team scalability.

I started with a research and investigation phase, benchmarking Figma’s capabilities against Illustrator specifically for technical diagramming. I engaged other Oracle teams who were already adopting Figma to understand their workflows and pain points.

Once the advantages became clear, I led the full transition by:

  • Re-architecting our diagram ecosystem into a Figma-based design system—including components, variants, asset libraries, shared color tokens, typography styles, and standardized templates.
  • Building a centralized component library that ensured every diagram stayed brand-consistent and technically accurate.
  • Developing governance rules for naming conventions, file structure, asset usage, and update workflows.
  • Creating training materials and leading workshops so designers could build confidence and proficiency in the new tool.
  • Implementing a phased rollout, ensuring that production work continued seamlessly during the transition.
  • Opening the system to other Oracle teams, enabling shared access, faster collaboration, and alignment across groups that previously worked in silos.

The shift to Figma fundamentally changed our operational efficiency and cross-team alignment:

  • Collaboration improved dramatically—teams across Oracle could co-edit, comment, and build on one shared design system.
  • Diagram production became significantly faster thanks to reusable components and real-time updates.
  • Brand consistency strengthened, as the Figma design system enforced standardized colors, iconography, and layouts.
  • Version control issues disappeared, saving designers hours of administrative time.
  • Our architecture diagram design system became a reusable asset that other organizations inside Oracle adopted, expanding the impact of our work well beyond our own team.

The project began as a cautious exploration but ultimately became one of the most impactful workflow upgrades we implemented — modernizing our diagramming pipeline, improving collaboration, and aligning our visual language across Oracle.

Instructional Video Modernization and Camtasia Ecosystem

Our team was relying on outdated ViewletBuilder software (Qarbon) to produce instructional videos. It lacked modern editing features, didn’t meet evolving accessibility requirements, and wasn’t compatible with our brand and security standards. As our content volume grew, the tool became a bottleneck that limited production quality and scalability.

I was responsible for evaluating and implementing a modernized video development solution. This included identifying the right tool, ensuring security and accessibility compliance, building a standardized design framework, and overseeing enterprise-wide adoption without disrupting ongoing production.

I led the transition to Camtasia (TechSmith), executing a comprehensive, multi-phase rollout:

  • Conducted a technical and instructional evaluation of Camtasia’s capabilities.
  • Navigated the CSSAP (Corporate Security Software Approval Process) to ensure the tool met corporate security and data protection requirements.
  • Secured funding and managed license procurement for our team.
  • Established accessibility standards aligned to WCAG—captions, font minimums, and color contrast guidelines.
  • Created brand-aligned templates, intros/outros, typography rules, color palettes, and callout styles.
  • Built a shared asset library for consistent visual elements.
  • Designed an end-to-end workflow covering scriptwriting, capturing, editing, review, and publishing.
  • Developed file naming conventions, version control rules, and a structured archival/retention system.
  • Provided training, best-practice documentation, tutorials, and checklists to accelerate skill adoption.
  • Implemented a standardized quality control and publishing process (1080p output + captioning).
  • Integrated the workflow into wiki pages and documentation portals for organizational visibility.

The transition modernized our entire instructional video ecosystem, resulting in improved content quality, greater security compliance, and enhanced accessibility. The standardized templates and workflows reduced production variability, improved efficiency, and enabled cross-functional teams to collaborate more effectively. The project became the foundation for scalable, brand-consistent video content across the organization.

SharePoint Migration and Knowledge Ecosystem Consolidation

Oracle had relied on OraDocs (Oracle Content Management) and Confluence for internal documentation and content storage. In 2024, Oracle announced an enterprise-wide transition to Microsoft OneDrive and SharePoint. This shift required every team to migrate their content, restructure their documentation approach, and align with new corporate standards—while ensuring no disruption to stakeholders who depended daily on our assets, diagrams, video files, and process documentation.

I was responsible for transitioning my team’s entire content ecosystem—file storage, diagrams, architecture documentation, wikis, and internal websites—to Microsoft OneDrive and SharePoint. My goal was not only to move files but to create a unified, intuitive, customer-facing hub where internal stakeholders could access our deliverables, processes, and standards from a single, reliable link.

  • Conducted a full audit of all existing assets across OraDocs, individual computers, and Confluence-based documentation.
  • Designed the information architecture for our new SharePoint sites, ensuring intuitive navigation, clear labeling, and alignment with our team workflows.
  • Migrated all files to OneDrive using a structured folder taxonomy to eliminate duplication, streamline access, and reduce search time.
  • Rebuilt documentation, process pages, and guidance from Confluence into SharePoint using modern pages, web parts, and integrated permissions.
  • Consolidated all customer-facing materials into one central SharePoint site, reducing confusion caused by fragmented links and outdated repositories.
  • Collaborated with IT and early-adopter teams to ensure compliance with security, access controls, and storage guidelines.
  • Trained my team on updated processes, file-management best practices, and how to maintain the SharePoint site going forward.

The migration resulted in a fully modernized, centralized content ecosystem that was delivered ahead of schedule with no interruption to customers or internal teams. By consolidating all processes, documentation, and deliverables into a single SharePoint site, I eliminated the confusion caused by scattered repositories and outdated links. The new structure significantly improved usability and reduced the time required to locate files and instructions by an estimated 40%. This unified hub enhanced transparency, strengthened alignment with Oracle’s new enterprise standards, and provided a scalable model that other teams began referencing for their own transitions.

Team Leadership and Operational Transformation

Team Integration and Business Continuity Program

When I joined the organization, the video development team and the graphic design team were operating in complete isolation. They had separate processes, tools, priorities, and communication channels. The lack of collaboration created bottlenecks, limited capacity, and—most critically—created single-points-of-failure. Both teams produced excellent work, but they functioned more like individual freelancers than a unified department. Leadership wanted stronger operational consistency, better scalability, and a more resilient workforce.

My task was to integrate these two unrelated teams into one cohesive creative organization. This required

  • Leading a change-management initiative
  • Building unified processes and communication rhythms
  • Establishing cross-training and skill redundancy
  • Creating a business continuity plan
  • Ensuring team culture evolved from “my work” to “our work”

I needed to strengthen operations, improve collaboration, and create a long-term strategic structure the organization could scale.

I approached the transformation in three structured phases:

Design & Align

  • Conducted a skills and workflow audit to understand strengths, gaps, and dependencies.
  • Met with each employee individually to understand pain points and form a shared vision.
  • Presented a strategic integration plan to leadership and secured buy-in.

Implement Structured Change Management

  • Combined the teams under a unified operating model with shared goals and expectations.
  • Introduced collaborative processes: joint planning meetings, shared project boards, and standardized review cycles.
  • Built a single design/video asset ecosystem—including templates, design systems, documentation, and naming conventions.
  • Launched cross-training tracks in animation, motion graphics, branding, and video editing to expand team flexibility.

Strengthen Long-Term Operations

  • Developed a business continuity plan covering skill redundancy, project handoff procedures, documentation, and succession planning.
  • Created capability matrices to prevent any single employee from becoming a bottleneck.
  • Established communication cadences—weekly syncs, shared Slack channels, and cross-team work reviews—to reinforce the new culture.

The impact was significant and measurable:

  • Transformed two siloed groups into a unified, cross-functional creative team that operated as a single strategic unit.
  • Improved throughput and delivery speed because work could be shared, handed off, and covered flexibly.
  • Eliminated single-points-of-failure, increasing operational resilience and reducing risk to critical timelines.
  • Expanded skill coverage across the team, enabling us to take on more complex multimedia projects.
  • Increased morale and engagement as employees saw growth opportunities and felt connected to a larger mission.

In the end, we created a scalable, resilient, and strategically aligned creative powerhouse—positioning the organization for higher output and future growth.

The documentation accelerated onboarding, reduced errors, and ensured brand and technical alignment across all deliverables.
